# Little Flame: first 30 minutes art brief

## Goal

Make the starting food truck a place the player believes has been worked in for years, then wants to improve. This is the first visual benchmark for the Old Market demo. Match the Alchemist's sense of authored construction and the reference RV's material and lighting quality without copying its layout or branding.

## Current diagnosis

The truck already has a usable silhouette and kitchen interactions. Its broad clean cream/teal/coral color regions, prominent roof billboard, evenly spaced awning bulbs, and isolated station props make it read as a decorated game object. The reference works because the shell, fittings, materials, light, and clutter all describe one functional lived-in space. More polygons alone will not close this gap.

## Exterior direction

Little Flame was once a city delivery van and has been converted, repaired, and repainted on a small budget. Keep the cream upper body and teal lower body as identity, but desaturate and break up the finish. A coral accent belongs around the serving hatch and a few hand-painted details, not a perfectly clean stripe on every surface.

- Fit the hatch into the body: visible frame, hinges, latches, counter lip, awning supports, seams, and a step at the entry.
- Replace the billboard feel with a smaller hand-painted name panel and a practical menu board at the service window. Keep the name readable from the sidewalk.
- Put wear where use explains it: chipped paint at door handles and step, grease below the vent, rust at lower seams and wheel arches, water marks below windows, patched metal at one corner. Preserve broad clean shapes so the truck does not become visual noise.
- Use mismatched replacement panels, a repaired awning edge, a cable or gas line, crates, a mat, and a small trash area to establish the conversion. These should not block paths or interactions.
- Keep one warm, inviting focal point at the serving hatch; the truck is battered, not abandoned.

## Working interior direction

Build a narrow, believable galley around the existing station locations. Preserve every current interaction point and reach distance. The player should understand where food travels: supplies to prep, grill, plating, window, then sink/trash.

- Join stations with continuous counters, lower cabinets, backsplash, shelving, and trim, instead of placing separate props against a bare shell.
- Give the grill an extractor hood, grease-marked metal behind it, and a task light. Give the sink faucet, drain board, dish rack, and worn splash zone. Give prep a cutting surface, towel hook, and shallow ingredient shelf.
- Use a scuffed non-slip floor, a narrow runner at the standing area, and small repairs to wall panels. Keep countertop clutter away from grab targets and sightlines.
- Layer soft daylight through the hatch with warm ceiling light and focused task light. At night the service hatch should glow onto the sidewalk, while the work surfaces remain legible.
- Check the interior from player eye height. The first view must show a connected working space and a clear route through it, not a row of props.

## Execution order and acceptance

1. Save fixed player-eye exterior, interior, and night comparison shots alongside the Alchemist benchmark.
2. Improve body construction and built-in galley first; assess silhouette and function with flat temporary materials.
3. Apply restrained material variation and localized wear. Reuse suitable owned POLYGON Shops props where they fit; make custom fitted panels where pack props cannot.
4. Light the service window, prep, grill, and sink as one space. Tune in-game exposure rather than judging only in an asset viewer.
5. Walk through the first service from a fresh save. Verify every workstation, doorway, and service interaction still works; repeat at night.

This pass is done when exterior and interior screenshots look intentionally authored at player eye height, the truck remains immediately readable as Little Flame, and the player can cook a full shift without art blocking or obscuring the workflow. Do this before expanding the Docks or adding more one-off scenery.
